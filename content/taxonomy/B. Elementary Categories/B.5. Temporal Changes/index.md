---
id: B.5
title: Temporal Changes
---

Cases subsumed in this {{< sc >}}Category{{< /sc >}} reference two temporal moments, t~~1~~ and t~~2~~, at which an agent’s epistemic standing could sensibly be evaluated and between which an epistemologically relevant change occurs. Changes to the following aspects are particularly relevant: p’s truth value, S’s basis for believing p, and S’s doxastic attitude towards p. As {{< sc >}}Temporal Changes{{< /sc >}} is an elementary Category, the kinds of ‘basis for belief’ are constituted by paradigmatic [case-types] of _knowledge_ or _lack thereof_ (see A.). Recall that S’s belief is _true_ in all paradigmatic knowledge [case-types] – [Clear Perception], [Accurate Memory] and [Correct Reasoning From True Premises] –; whereas there are three paradigmatic lack of knowledge [case-types] in which S’s belief is _false_ – [Misperception], [Misremembering] and [False Reasoning] – but also one where S’s belief is _true_ – [Unsuitable Reason]. Typically, in tokens of the [types] subsumed by {{< sc >}}Temporal Changes{{< /sc >}}, p will be a proposition whose truth value can change because it involves an indexical referring to the time of utterance, so that S can consistently believe that ‘p is true _now_’ at t~~1~~ and that ‘p is false _now_’ at t~~2~~. For this reason, I exclude instances of [Accurate Memory] and [Misremembering] as a possible basis for beliefs (see also the comments for these [case-types] in A.). Similarly, instances of [Correct Reasoning From True Premises] and [False Reasoning] which are not based on experience at all are excluded. To indicate this, I use the phrases “[Clear Perception] and/or [Correct Reasoning From True Premises] relating to what S perceives or remembers in the moment” and “[Misperception] and/or [Fale Reasoning] relating to what S perceives or remembers in the moment.” The [types] in which p’s truth value remains constant are an exemption to this rule: In some of their tokens, proposition p may be a proposition whose truth value _couldn’t_ change (rather than incidentally doesn’t change). The same applies in complex {{< sc >}}Categories{{< /sc >}} involving this complicating factor.

I make the simplifying assumptions that S’s doxastic attitude is exclusively either ‘believing that p (is true)’ or ‘believing that non-p (is true)’; and S’s doxastic attitude can’t change without a change in p’s truth value and/or S’s basis for belief. – I recognise that doxastic attitudes are, in reality, more complex: Agents can ‘not believe anything about p,’ ‘be agnostic about whether p,’ ‘believe that p is not true without therefore believing that non-p must be true,’ etc. Furthermore, I equate ‘p being true’ with ‘non-p being false’, and ‘non-p being true’ with ‘p being false’ for exegetical purposes. Additionally, I assume that the variable ‘p’ can stand for negated propositions.

Moreover, I presume that a change in p’s truth value always entails that there is a change in S’s basis for belief and/or doxastic attitude: If p is true at t~~1~~ and S believes that p, then S believes based on [Clear Perception], [Accurate Memory] and/or [Correct Reasoning from True Premises] or on [Unsuitable Reason] at t~~1~~. When a change in truth value occurs, making p false at t~~2~~, S _cannot_ believe p on the same basis as at t~~2~~, since _p is now false_. If p is false at t~~1~~, but S believes that p, then S believes p based on [Misperception], [Misremembering] and/or [False Reasoning] at t~~1~~. When a change in truth value occurs, making p true at t~~2~~, S _cannot_ believe p on the same basis at t~~2~~, since _p is now true_.{{< fn 1 "If S bases the belief that p on remembering the evidence S had for believing this at t~1~ and the false premise that nothing interfered with the matter of fact described by p in between of t~1~ and t~2~, then S’s belief is *gettiered*, see C.8. {{< sc >}}Truth From False Premise + Temporal Changes.{{< /sc >}})" >}}

These simplifying assumptions leave four kinds of {{< sc >}}Temporal Changes{{< /sc >}} to consider: (i.) a change of S’s basis for belief; (ii.) a change of S’s basis for belief and S’s doxastic attitude towards p; (iii.) a change of p’s truth value and of S’s basis for belief; and (iv.) a change of p’s truth value, S’s basis for belief and S’s doxastic attitude towards p.
