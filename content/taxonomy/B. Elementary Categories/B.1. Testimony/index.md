title: Testimony
---

Giving and receiving (spoken or written) testimony is an important source of knowledge in our day-to-day lives. It is generally assumed that the key factors for gaining knowledge through testimony are (i.) a trustworthy and reliable _attester_ (at least regarding the relevant subject); (ii.) a _transmission-process_ that does not distort the information; (iii.) an attentive and critical _receiver_, who identifies suitable attesters correctly and for the right reasons, monitors for counterevidence and draws appropriate conclusions. Attesters often assert information that is itself obtained through testimony. Consequently, cases regularly take the form of _testimonial chains_ of varying lengths; attester R~~1~~ attesting that p to R~~2~~, R~~2~~ to… R~~n-1~~, R~~n 1~~ to R~~n~~, with R~~n~~ finally attesting this to S. For simplicity, the following [case types] are formulated such that only the last step of such chains (R~~n~~ attesting to S) is considered.
