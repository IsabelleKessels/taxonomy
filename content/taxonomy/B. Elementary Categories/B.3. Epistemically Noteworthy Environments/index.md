id: B.3
title: Epistemically Noteworthy Environments
---

This {{< sc >}}Category{{< /sc >}} subsumes cases where S forms a true belief that p, but S’s environment is noteworthy in regards to the evidence it contains for the matter of fact(s) described by p (or by p-type propositions). S is unaware of this. Pritchard (2009) coined the term “epistemically unfriendly environment” (64) to describe the situation in Goldman’s (1976) iconic Fake Barns case (credited to Carl Ginet), and I adopt this label for the corresponding [case type]: S’s environment contains several pieces of _misleading evidence_ in regards to p. Through sheer luck, S doesn’t encounter this misleading evidence in the @ world.{{< fn 1 "Pritchard (2009) uses the term “environmental epistemic luck” (64) for the phenomenon described here, see also the introductions to B.2., fn. 2 and B.4., fn. 1." >}} The implication is that S would encounter the misleading evidence and form a false belief about p in close possible worlds.{{< fn 2 "These counterfactual-scenarios would be instances of [Misperception] and/or [False Reasoning], where the misleading evidence functions as a false premise of S’s reasoning process." >}} The misleading evidence can take the form of facsimiles of the object, or a doppelgänger/twin of the person, that the belief p is about, or evidence through broken or unreliable instruments. Tokens differ in:

- the likelihood of perceiving the misleading evidence, i.e., its spatial-temporal proximity to the agent, {{< fn 3 "Most cases in the literature avoid extremes, but for detailed variants of Fake Barns where it is extremely unlikely that S will encounter a façade, see Lackey’s (2006) Southernmost Barn (288) and Grundmann’s (2020) Southernmost Residence (5167). Gendler & Hawthorne (2005) explore further variations of [Unfriendly Environment] cases in which agents see a real diamond in an environment which contains fake diamonds in different spatial-temporal proximities." >}}
- the ratio of misleading to non-misleading evidence,{{< fn 4 "Most cases in the literature avoid extremes, but for detailed variants of Fake Barns where it is extremely unlikely that S will encounter a façade, see Lackey’s (2006) Southernmost Barn (288) and Grundmann’s (2020) Southernmost Residence (5167). Gendler & Hawthorne (2005) explore further variations of [Unfriendly Environment] cases in which agents see a real diamond in an environment which contains fake diamonds in different spatial-temporal proximities." >}}
- the persuasiveness of the misleading evidence, i.e., how easily a lay person or expert would detect it, etc.

Since each factor encompasses different degrees of application, [type]-formulations evoking uniform evaluation-behaviour may require the formulation of [sub-types].

Next to [Unfriendly Environment] containing misleading evidence, I additionally group cases under {{< sc >}}Epistemically Noteworthy Environments{{< /sc >}}, wherein some kind of _unexpected regularity_ would provide agents additional evidence for p, if they were aware of it. This complication, alone, may be uninteresting, but it combines with other complicating factors (e.g., with {{< sc >}}Truth From False Premise{{< /sc >}}, see C.6.) to create new [case-types], which subsume tokens-cases discussed in the armchair literature.
