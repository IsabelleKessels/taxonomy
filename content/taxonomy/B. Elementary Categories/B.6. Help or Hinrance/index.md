title: Help or Hindrance
---

{{< sc >}}HELP OR HINDRANCE{{< /sc >}} subsumes [case-types] in which external factors manipulate agents’ belief-formation processes, cognitive states, sensory organs and/or perceptual processes. This manipulation can have a positive (helpful) or a negative (hindering) effect. Additionally, this {{< sc >}}CATEGORY{{< /sc >}} also subsumes cases in which agents go through an atypical cognitive process, which I classify as [Cognitive Outlier] cases. The overarching similarity between all [types] in this {{< sc >}}CATEGORY{{< /sc >}} is that something is remarkable about the agent’s cognitive or sensory process.

In some [case-types], the cognitive process is directly manipulated: Agents are artificially _set into the cognitive state of believing p_ by a demon with mind-altering powers, a tampering neuro surgeon, or some more prosaic intervener (undetected brain lesion, hallucinogenic drug, etc.). Most of these examples are purely speculative, but nonetheless common in the philosophical literature. It’s easier to imagine quotidian scenarios for manipulations to agents’ sensory processes, or more indirect influences on their cognitive processes: e.g., when agents temporarily have worse hearing after an acoustic trauma, or they find it easier to concentrate after ingesting stimulating substances like Adderall, or harder to do so after ingesting narcotics. I suggest additionally including tokens in which external circumstances are (un)favourable to accurate belief-formation, e.g., by making it considerably easier or harder to concentrate (utilising a low-stimulus environment when taking an exam could be classified as setting favourable outer conditions). Tokens differ in:

- whether help or hindrance is administered systematically or sporadically,
- whether all, some subset, or only one of the agent’s beliefs is helped/hindered.
- whether S is aware of the influence and has some level of control this.

Since these factors plausibly impact evaluation-behaviour, establishing [type]-formulations evoking uniform evaluation-behaviour might require the formulation of [sub-types].
