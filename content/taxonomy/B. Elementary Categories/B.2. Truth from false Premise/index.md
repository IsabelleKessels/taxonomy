The {{< sc >}}CATEGORY{{< /sc >}} subsumes standard ‘Gettier cases’ on the one hand and ‘Knowledge From Falsehood cases’ on the other, in virtue of them sharing the similarity that the agent gains a true belief p based on a false premise q. The label ‘Gettier case’ is often applied loosely to cover all cases where armchair philosophers judge that the agent has a _justified true belief_ but _falls short of knowledge_. I work with a restricted understanding of a ‘Gettier case’, concentrating on its elementary form: In the @-world some _misleading evidence_ for q is present in S’s environment.{{< fn 1 "S believes that q based on [Misperception], [Misremembering] and/or [False Reasoning]. Technically, misremembers or making a reasoning mistake, requires no misleading evidence to be actually ‘*present* in S’s *environment*’, however, I here take S’s apparent memory or reasoning mistake to constitute *misleading evidence* for the sake of simplicity." >}} If q is true, this entails that p (and that a specific matter of fact, f~~1~~, functioning as p’s truth-maker); if q is false, p’s truth is indeterminate. S encounters this misleading evidence, and thus judges that q is true, even though q is false. S either explicitly forms the false belief that q and then infers that p, or directly forms the entailed belief that p (in the latter case, q would only be cited as a premise by the agent, when prompted to explain how they arrived at the belief p). S has no other evidence for believing that p. As it so happens, there is a ‘_backup truth-maker,’_ f~~2~~, for p. This backup truth-maker f~~2~~ is some matter of fact rendering p true which: (i.) S is unaware of; and (ii.) is independent of and district from f~~1~~. The second condition has an important implication: It means that there are close possible worlds in which the same misleading evidence would be present in S’s environment but _without_ the backup truth-maker. S would encounter this misleading evidence and, based on this, form the belief that p~~1~~, but here S’s belief that p would be false.{{< fn 2 "Pritchard suggests the label “intervening epistemic luck” (Pritchard 2015, 105) for this situation, because “luck ‘intervenes’ between belief and fact” (Pritchard 2009, 143), see also the introductions to B.3., fn.1 and B.4., fn. 1." >}}

The ‘Knowledge Form Falsehood’ cases have a very similar set-up, but differ in the second condition: Here, the backup truth-maker and the misleading evidence for q are _interdependent_, such that, if the truth-maker were absent, so would the misleading evidence.

I distinguish two main [types] of the ‘Gettier’ variety – [False Premise Gettier] and [Direct Gettier] – which differ in whether the agent explicitly forms the _false belief_ q in their reasoning process. I omit this distinction for ‘Knowledge From Falsehood,’ since cases of this structure in the literature typically feature an agent who explicitly endorses the false premise q, and I am unaware of thought experiments where the false premise is only implicitly held. Beyond this, I further group [case-types] in which the agent forms their belief by reading an instrument{{< fn 4 "The term ‘instrument’ is primarily associated with physical instruments, e.g., clocks (tracking time in a specific time zone), thermometers (tracking temperature), Geiger counters (tracking radiation), etc. – and this is how the [Instrument …] [case-types] I mention are formulated. Nonetheless, ‘intellectual instruments’, say an algorithm I use to solve a maths problem, are broadly analogous. The right algorithm should correctly ‘track’ inputs and solutions. Both physical and intellectual instruments can be reliable or unreliable. Goldberg (2012a) provides an interesting discussion of the differences and similarities between belief-formation based on *testimony* and *instrument* use. One could argue that *forming beliefs by interpreting instruments* should itself constitute an elementary {{< sc >}}Category ‘Instrument.’ {{< /sc >}} I decided against this, as it would unduly complicate this taxonomy. Instead, I identify instrument-variants within {{< sc >}}Truth From False Premise, Unfriendly Environment{{< /sc >}} and {{< sc >}}Counterfactual-interference{{< /sc >}} and in derivate complex {{< sc >}}Categories{{< /sc >}}." >}} under this {{< sc >}}CATEGORY{{< /sc >}}.

Tokens of all [types] in this {{< sc >}}CATEGORY{{< /sc >}} will differ in the probability that S encounters misleading evidence or misperceives something. Tokens for the [case-types] of the ‘Gettier’ variety will additionally differ in the probability that a backup truth-maker for p is present. Since these factors encompass different degrees of application, [type]-formulations evoking uniform evaluation-behaviour may require the formulation of [sub-types].
