By providing three examples of third-level case-complexity cases, together with the second-level complexity cases presented in C., I hope to achieve two goals: Firstly, to illustrate that combinations of elementary {{< sc >}}Categories{{< /sc >}} _produce relevant complex {{< sc >}}Categories{{< /sc >}}_: i.e., {{< sc >}}Categories{{< /sc >}} that subsume thought experiments discussed in the literature. Secondly, and relatedly, to convince readers that significant differences in case-complexity levels exist among thought experiments levered in armchair argumentation about theories of knowledge. All of this, ultimately, serves to support one key claim of my book _Operationalising Theory-Construction_: A thought experiment’s case-complexity can (and should) play a significant role in assessing the evidential weight granted to our intuition about it; and, hence, the role it should play in theory-construction and testing.
