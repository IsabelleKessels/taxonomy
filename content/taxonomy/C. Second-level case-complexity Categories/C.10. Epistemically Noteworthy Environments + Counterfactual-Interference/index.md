title: Epistemically Noteworthy Environments + Counterfactual-Interference
---

In {{< sc >}}Counterfactual-Interference{{< /sc >}} cases S gains a true belief based on _prima facie_ suitable evidence in the @-world, but there would be an interference in modally close possible worlds such that S would gain a false belief. By itself, the modal profile of {{< sc >}}Counterfactual-Interference{{< /sc >}} cases is such that S gains a true belief based on _prima facie_ suitable evidence in the @-world – and no misleading evidence is present in their environment. In modally close possible worlds, however, S would gain a false belief based on misleading evidence because of the interference. For {{< sc >}}Epistemically Noteworthy Environments{{< /sc >}}, I suggested that this complicating factor can take two forms: environments can be noteworthy because they are _unfriendly_ or contain an _unexpected regularity_. In the former, the agent’s environment contains misleading evidence in regards to p, but S doesn’t encounter this misleading evidence and bases their belief on, _prima facie_, suitable evidence in @-world. The implication is that S would gain a false belief based on the misleading evidence in modally close possible worlds. This is combinable with {{< sc >}}Counterfactual-Interference{{< /sc >}} in two ways:{{< fn 1 "In this preliminary taxonomy, I don’t differentiate [case-types] in this complex {{< sc >}}Category{{< /sc >}} by whether the _unfriendly environment_ manifests as [Unfriendly Environment] or [Unfriendly Instruments Environment]; and whether {{< sc >}}Counterfactual-Interference{{< /sc >}} manifests as [Counterfactual-Interference in Modally Close non-p-Worlds] or [Instrument Counterfactual-Interference in Modally Close non-p-Worlds], or as [Counterfactual-Interference in Modally Close p-Worlds] or [Instrument Counterfactual-Interference in Modally Close p-Worlds], respectively. Separate [case-types] can (and should) be established _if_ x-phi results on tokens of the corresponding elementary [types] indicate different evaluation-behaviours." >}}

- The environment is normal in the @-world, but a counterfactual-interference would turn the environment into an unfriendly one in modally close possible worlds.
- The environment is unfriendly in the @-world, but a counterfactual-interference would moderate the environment’s effects in modally close p-worlds (to make the counterfactual-environment more _accommodating_ to some degree or other).

It’s less clear how _unexpected regularities_ could be combined with {{< sc >}}Counterfactual-Interference{{< /sc >}}. In the elementary [Unexpected Regularity Environment], S’s environment features a regularity regarding the relationship of p and q; such that (if S recognised the regularity) evidence for q-type beliefs represent good evidence for p-type beliefs. S, however, is ignorant of this. The decisive factors are the presence of a regularity and S’s ignorance. The elementary {{< sc >}}Counterfactual-Interference{{< /sc >}} already features a different kind of regularity, albeit one that could only manifest counterfactually. That an interference with evidence for p-type propositions (and potentially the matter of fact described by p) _would_ occur in (most) modally close possible worlds amounts to a regularity; and this regularity is unexpected by S. Given this understanding, I struggle to identify a distinct [case-type] that might amount to a combination of [Unexpected Regularity Environment] and {{< sc >}}Counterfactual-Interference{{< /sc >}}.
