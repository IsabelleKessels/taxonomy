---
id: C.6
title: Truth From False Premise + Epistemically Noteworthy Environments
---

The complicating factor of {{< sc >}}Truth From False Premise{{< /sc >}} is characterised by S encountering misleading evidence on which they (explicitly or implicitly) form a false belief q, from which they infer a belief p made accurate by some (independent or interdependent) backup truth-maker. By itself, the modal profile of {{< sc >}}Truth From False Premise{{< /sc >}} cases which amount to _‘gettiering’_ is such that the presence of misleading evidence and a backup truth-maker happens in the @-world, but S would gain a false belief that p in modally close possible worlds, wherein the backup truth-maker wouldn’t be present, but the misleading evidence for q would be. In the ‘Knowledge From Falsehood’ variety of {{< sc >}}Truth From False Premise{{< /sc >}} cases the backup truth-maker’s presence is interdependent on the presence of the misleading evidence, so that the misleading evidence wouldn’t be present in close possible worlds where the backup truth-maker wouldn’t be present.

For {{< sc >}}Epistemically Noteworthy Environments{{< /sc >}}, I suggested that this complicating factor can take two forms: environments can be noteworthy because they are _unfriendly_ or contain an _unexpected regularity_. In the former, the agent’s environment contains misleading evidence in regards to p, but S doesn’t encounter this misleading evidence and bases their belief on, _prima facie_, suitable evidence in @-world. The implication is that S would gain a false belief based on the misleading evidence in modally close possible worlds. There are limited ways to combine this with Truth From False Premise, since these two complications conflict: [Unfriendly Environment] requires S not to base their belief on the misleading evidence; Truth From False Premise requires S’s belief be based on misleading evidence (or a false premise). I hold that the only way to integrate these factors is to implement _gettiering_ in the modally close possible worlds of an unfriendly environment scenario, see [Almost Gettiered in Unfriendly Environment]. I resort to call this _‘gettiering,’_ because when mixed with another complication that has implication on a case’s modal profile, it becomes difficult to differentiate whether the way that {{< sc >}}Truth From False Premise{{< /sc >}} is implemented lines up more closely with the ‘Standard Gettier’ or ‘Knowledge From Falsehood’ variety. In those instances, I resort to the term ‘gettering’ by default (see the introduction to B.2., fn. 3).

In contrast, the latter way of implementing {{< sc >}}Epistemically Noteworthy Environments{{< /sc >}}, with an _unexpected regularity_, facilitates combination with {{< sc >}}Truth From False Premise{{< /sc >}} unproblematically: In the elementary [Unexpected Regularity Environment], S’s environment features a regularity regarding the relationship of p and q; such that (if S recognised the regularity) evidence for q-type beliefs represent good evidence for p-type beliefs. S, however, is ignorant of this. The decisive factors are the presence of a regularity and S’s ignorance. When combined with {{< sc >}}Truth From False Premise{{< /sc >}}, we receive [case-types] in which S believes something based on false assumptions, but an environmental regularity guarantees a backup truth-maker both in the @-world and modally close possible worlds. Consequently, we can end up with [case-types] which look akin to the more elementary [Knowledge From Falsehood], just that the interdependence between backup truth-maker and false premise amounts to a localised phenomenon specific to this epistemic environment. Nonetheless, I by default call this _‘gettiering’_ in the [case-type]-titles in question.
