To uncover patterns amongst thought experiments on knowledge, this taxonomy identifies **{{< sc >}}Categories{{< /sc >}}** of complicating factors which serve as common ingredients across complex hypothetical cases. This enables us to distinguish different levels of case-complexity among thought experiments: Some paradigmatic cases of _knowledge_ (and _lack thereof_) involve no complicating factors (see section A.). Cases involving a single complicating factor are grouped under respective elementary {{< sc >}}Categories{{< /sc >}} (see section B.). If several factors are combined in one hypothetical scenario, the case in question belongs in a complex {{< sc >}}Category{{< /sc >}} (see section C. and D. for examples).

Within any given Category, the taxonomy further identifies **[case-types]**. These group together thought experiments with _analogous structures_, while abstracting away from illustrative details. Information on a [case-type] is presented like this:

|[case-type]-formulation|Token-cases|Commentary|
|-|-|-|
|A formulation of the hypothesised relevant structural features shared by all tokens of this [type].|Fully formulated thought experiments of this [type]. Where available, these cases are taken from the philosophical literature.|The stated intuition of token-cases’ authors; x‑phi data on the token; reflections on the taxonomising process, etc.|
