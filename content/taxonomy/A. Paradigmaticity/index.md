{{< sc >}}Paradigmaticity{{< /sc >}} subsumes _paradigmatic cases of knowledge_ and _lack of knowledge_. I defend my rationale for choosing which [case-types] to subsume under this {{< sc >}}Category{{< /sc >}} in _Operationalising Theory-Construction_ (specifically in subchapter 4.3.1.). For the purposes of this taxonomy, I provisionally accept the assumption that knowledge is factive; i.e., I presume it uncontroversial that S _lacks knowledge_ in tokens of [types] where S gains a _false belief_.{{< fn 1 "Turri, Buckwalter, & Blouw’s (2015) x-phi study includes a case where “a protagonist with a false belief was overwhelmingly viewed as being ignorant, even when the false belief was eminently reasonable (Ignorance Control)” (386). They reason: “This supports a widespread assumption […] – namely, that the concept of knowledge is ‘factive,’ meaning that its application presupposes that the claim known is true. We think an interpretation based on factivity is the simplest and most consistent with prior findings on knowledge attributions. Nevertheless, some caution is warranted here, because the results are consistent with more complicated alternative interpretations of the data that reject factivity.” (386.) I don’t state and classify the vignette Turri et al. (2015) used because it is formulated too ambiguously to discern whether it should be subsumed under the paradigmatic [False Reasoning] or under B.1., [False Testimony]: A second agent R is involved in the case, and it’s unclear whether R attests a falsehood to S, or doesn’t prevent S from forming a belief based on false premises by withholding relevant information." >}}
